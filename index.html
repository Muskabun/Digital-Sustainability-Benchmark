User actions

What is the user trying to do?

The agency wants to reduce the environmental impact of its digital work and visits the landing page of the Digital Sustainability Benchmark Tool.

Selects Start benchmark, reads how the process works, picks a compliance framework if relevant, and chooses a path: go straight to the tool or learn more first.

Finance, operations, design and development complete the questionnaire together, scoring the project across five values: Clean, Efficiency, Stewardship, Non-manipulative design, Lifecycle.

Reviews the sustainability score as a spider chart, reads what each score means and compares it to the benchmark.

Reads role-based recommendations, picks what to apply, downloads the benchmark report and brings it to Friday's client kick-off.

Positive feedback

What is working? What makes the user happy?

The landing page is visually strong and immediately catches attention. The explanation of digital sustainability looks clear and approachable.

Insights

What does the data tell us?

The first screen does not make the purpose, business value and outcome explicit enough. Users need digital sustainability explained in practical agency terms before they commit time.

The tool works at project level and at kick-off, when decisions are cheapest to change. This has to be said upfront, or users assess the wrong thing.

Knowledge is spread across roles. What one person knows is not what the team knows. Most usability problems were in the copy, not the layout.

A score without context is meaningless. Users need explanations and a benchmark to know what “good” looks like.

The score is a starting point. Real value is in the conversation it starts, and in giving teams a reason to come back.

Opportunities

  • Lead with a value proposition: “Measure where your agency stands today and get practical actions to make your digital work more sustainable.”
  • A short, concrete definition: energy use, efficient design and development, digital waste, responsible tech choices.
  • Show the outcome upfront: score, visual report, tailored actions, progress tracking.
  • State clearly: one project, assessed at kick-off.
  • Ask the team to name the project before starting.
  • Show the steps: Define → Score → Act → Certify.
  • Show “X minutes to discuss with your team” per section.
  • Prompt users to check uncertain answers with the relevant role.
  • Keep reviewing questions for neutrality.
  • Pair every score with a plain-language explanation and an industry benchmark.
  • Highlight the one or two values to discuss first.
  • Map results to certifications: “You're aligned with X, here's what to change.”
  • Re-benchmark at project milestones to track progress over time.

Service blueprint

What happens in front of and behind the user at each phase

Line of interaction

Frontstage

What the user sees and interacts with

Framer landing page “Digital doesn't mean invisible”: hero, definition, five core values, primary CTA.

Onboarding screens explaining the evaluate → reflect flow, framework selection and the choice of depth.

Question screens grouped by value, with examples, an “I don't know” option and progress indicator.

Results screen: spider chart across five values, with score explanations and benchmark.

Recommendations filtered by role (designer, developer, finance, sales…) and a downloadable benchmark report.

Line of visibility

Backstage

What the system and team do out of sight

Content strategy translates sustainability principles into agency language and business benefits: cost savings, efficiency, profit.

Selected framework and chosen depth configure which questions and how much guidance appear.

Answers are saved per value; “I don't know” answers are flagged instead of scored as low.

Scoring logic aggregates answers into five value scores and plots them against the benchmark.

Low-scoring values are mapped to role-specific actions; the report is generated from scores and chosen actions.

Line of internal interaction

Support processes

Knowledge, partners and research behind the service

Positioning tested with MDD students, educators and Faraday. Landing page built in Framer.

Compliance frameworks and certification criteria referenced for framework selection.

Question bank built on W3C Web Sustainability Guidelines, Sustainable Web Manifesto, Wholegrain Digital methods and UN SDGs; reviewed for neutrality.

Faraday's existing spider-chart project evaluation used as the result format. Benchmark data from assessed projects.

Recommendation library per role, based on Faraday's 9 roles and responsibilities.

How we got here

  • Pivot 1. A values workshop: a blank page was too open.
  • Pivot 2. Workshop 2.0, then a digital tool, after Faraday asked “Who is going to use it?”
  • Pivot 3. Benchmark projects, not company values.

Biggest lesson

Ask “who uses it, and how often?” in week one. Two of three pivots came from that question, and the score only matters for the conversation it starts.
